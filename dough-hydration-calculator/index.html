How Baker's Percentages & True Hydration Work

What is a Baker's Percentage?

In baking, standard domestic volume measurements (cups, tablespoons) are notoriously inaccurate because flour settles and compacts. Instead, professional bakers use Baker's Percentages, where the Total Flour Weight is always set to 100.0%. Every other ingredient (water, salt, starter, yeast) is expressed as a direct percentage of that flour weight.

For instance, if your recipe calls for 500 grams of flour and 75% hydration, you need exactly 500 × 0.75 = 375 grams of water. If you need 2% salt, you need 500 × 0.02 = 10 grams of salt.

The Sourdough "Starter Trap" (True Hydration)

Most domestic sourdough bakers make a critical calculation mistake: they add starter to the recipe without adjusting their water and flour.

A standard 100% hydration sourdough starter consists of equal parts flour and water (50% flour, 50% water by weight). If you add 100 grams of starter to your dough, you are quietly adding 50 grams of uncounted water and 50 grams of uncounted flour. If you do not subtract that water from your liquid addition, your dough becomes noticeably wetter and more slack than intended, often causing loaves to spread flat during baking.

SpinFoundry's True Hydration formula solves this mathematically:

  • FlourFromStarter = StarterWeight / (1 + StarterHydrationRatio)
  • WaterFromStarter = (StarterWeight × StarterHydrationRatio) / (1 + StarterHydrationRatio)
  • FlourToAddDirectly = TotalFlour - FlourFromStarter
  • WaterToAddDirectly = TotalWaterDesired - WaterFromStarter

Typical Hydration Guidelines

  • 50% – 58%: Stiff doughs (Bagels, pretzels, pasta).
  • 60% – 66%: Standard table bread, French baguettes, sandwich loaves.
  • 70% – 78%: Artisan sourdough country loaves with open, custardy crumb.
  • 80% – 90%+: Ciabatta, pan de cristal, high-hydration focaccia.
