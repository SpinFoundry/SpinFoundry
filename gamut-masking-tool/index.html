What is Gamut Masking?

Popularized by master painter and author James Gurney (Color and Light), gamut masking is a disciplined method for establishing color harmony. By cutting a geometric window in paper and laying it over a color wheel, the artist restricts the palette to a specific chromatic neighborhood. This creates unified atmosphere, mood, and color relationships while eliminating accidental disharmony.

Why Value Preservation is Critical

In painting, "Value does all the work, but color gets all the credit." Tonal value (perceptual luminance) dictates depth, form, lighting, and readability. Standard RGB or HSL color shifts often drastically distort lightness (e.g., desaturating yellow causes it to darken into an olive brown, while desaturating navy blue lightens it).

This tool executes all calculations in OKLCH (a cylindrical representation of the perceptual OKLab color space). Lightness (\(L\)) is mathematically decoupled from chroma (\(C\)) and hue (\(h\)). When an out-of-gamut pixel is projected onto the nearest boundary of the mask polygon, its original luminance \(L\) remains 100% untouched.

How to Use This in Your Studio Workflow

  • Mood Color Scripts: Drag the rotation slider to instantly transpose a daylight scene into a golden hour sunset or cool moonlight ambiance without losing lighting values.
  • Reference Harmonization: Feed heterogeneous photo references through a single gamut mask before painting to guarantee that background and character elements share the exact same chromatic world.
  • Palette Generation: Use the live extracted swatch panel below the wheel to populate your digital painting mixer brush or traditional oil palette.
